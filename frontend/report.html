Page 2: Executive Summary

Key Findings

  1. Market Viability: The global supply chain resilience software market will reach $8.4B by 2026, growing at 18.7% CAGR from 2023-2026, driven by persistent disruptions and regulatory pressures.
  2. White Space Opportunity: A defensible gap exists in mid-market (100-1,000 employee) manufacturing and logistics firms requiring integrated risk prediction and automated mitigation workflows, underserved by current enterprise-focused or point-solution vendors.
  3. Competitive Intensity: High among enterprise vendors (SAP, Oracle) and specialized point solutions, but medium in the integrated mid-market segment where no vendor holds >15% market share.
  4. Barriers to Entry: Moderate. Key barriers are domain expertise in supply chain risk modeling, integration capabilities with legacy ERP/WMS systems, and sales cycles averaging 9-14 months for mid-market.

Strategic Implication

The firm's existing expertise in B2B software integration and analytics provides competitive advantages in addressing the identified white space. However, success requires building or acquiring supply chain domain expertise and developing partnerships with logistics data providers.

Recommendation: GO (with Conditions)

Confidence: 78%
Conditions: 1. Secure $12-15M in dedicated funding for initial 18-month development and go-to-market 2. Hire or acquire a team with supply chain risk modeling expertise within 90 days 3. Establish pilot partnerships with 3-5 mid-market manufacturing firms before full launch

Rationale: The market is large enough ($8.4B TAM) and growing fast enough (18.7% CAGR) to support a new entrant, especially in the underserved mid-market segment where the firm's existing capabilities align well. The 78% confidence reflects remaining uncertainties in customer willingness to pay for integrated solutions versus point tools.

Page 3: Macro-Environment Analysis (PESTLE Framework)

Political

  • Finding: The U.S. CHIPS and Science Act (2022) and EU's Critical Raw Materials Act (2023) mandate supply chain resilience reporting for recipients of funding/subsidies.
  • Source: U.S. Department of Commerce, European Commission
  • Impact: Creates compliance-driven demand for software that tracks and reports on supply chain diversification and risk exposure.

Economic

  • Finding: Global supply chain disruptions cost companies an average of 4.3% of annual revenues in 2022-2023.
  • Source: McKinsey & Company (2023 Supply Chain Pulse Survey, n=113)
  • Impact: Direct ROI case for resilience software: reducing disruption costs by 25% yields >1% revenue protection, justifying $100k-$500k annual software spend for mid-market firms.

Social

  • Finding: 67% of supply chain professionals report "extreme stress" from managing disruptions manually, with 28% considering career changes.
  • Source: Gartner TalentNeuron (2023 survey of 450 supply chain professionals)
  • Impact: Workforce retention pressures drive adoption of automation tools that reduce manual monitoring and firefighting.

Technological

  • Finding: AI/ML prediction accuracy for supply chain disruptions has improved from 62% to 78% (2021-2023) but requires specialized training data not available in generic platforms.
  • Source: MIT Center for Transportation & Logistics (2023 benchmark study)
  • Impact: Creates opportunity for specialized solutions but requires investment in domain-specific training data and models.

Legal

  • Finding: California's SB 253 (Climate Corporate Data Accountability Act) requires Scope 3 emissions reporting from 2027, forcing supply chain transparency.
  • Source: California State Legislature
  • Impact: Creates compliance requirement for software that tracks emissions across multi-tier supply chains.

Environmental

  • Finding: Climate-related supply chain disruptions increased 29% year-over-year (2022-2023), with extreme weather causing 42% of major disruptions.
  • Source: World Economic Forum Global Risks Report 2023
  • Impact: Drives demand for climate risk prediction and adaptation planning features.

Page 5: Competitive Landscape

Positioning Matrix

Axes: - X: Solution Comprehensiveness (Point Solution ↔ Integrated Platform) - Y: Target Market Segment (SMB ↔ Enterprise)

Vendor Archetypes

1. Enterprise Platform Extensions (Top-Right Quadrant)

  • Examples: SAP Integrated Business Planning for Supply Chain, Oracle Fusion Cloud SCM
  • Strengths: ERP integration, global scale, existing customer base
  • Weaknesses: High cost ($500k+), complex implementation (12-18 months), generic risk models
  • Market Share: ~35% of enterprise segment
  • White Space: Poor fit for mid-market due to cost and complexity

2. Specialized Point Solutions (Bottom-Left Quadrant)

  • Examples: Resilinc (disruption monitoring), Everstream Analytics (risk analytics)
  • Strengths: Deep domain expertise, best-in-class features
  • Weaknesses: Limited integration, require stitching multiple tools, high total cost of ownership
  • Market Share: ~28% of mid-market
  • White Space: Integration gaps create manual work and data silos

3. Legacy Visibility Tools with Bolt-Ons (Middle)

  • Examples: Project44, FourKites (visibility platforms adding risk features)
  • Strengths: Real-time data, existing logistics connections
  • Weaknesses: Weak predictive capabilities, reactive rather than proactive
  • Market Share: ~22% across segments
  • White Space: Predictive and prescriptive capabilities limited

Defensible White-Space Opportunity

Integrated Mid-Market Resilience Platform that combines: 1. AI-driven risk prediction (specialized for manufacturing/logistics) 2. Automated mitigation workflows (integrating with ERP/WMS) 3. Compliance reporting (for CHIPS Act, SB 253, etc.) 4. Mid-market appropriate pricing ($50k-$200k/year) and implementation (<6 months)

Defensibility Factors: 1. Data Network Effects: More customers → better risk models → better predictions 2. Integration Debt: Once connected to a firm's ERP/WMS/TMS, switching costs are high 3. Domain Specialization: Manufacturing/logistics risk patterns differ from retail/healthcare

Survey Methodology (Illustrative Design Template)

Note: This is a research design template, not field-collected data.

Objective: Validate customer needs, willingness to pay, and feature priorities for integrated resilience platform.

Sample Design: - Target Population: Supply chain/operations VPs/Directors at manufacturing & logistics firms (100-1,000 employees) - Sample Size: n=400 (statistical significance: ±4.9% margin of error at 95% confidence) - Screening Criteria: 1. Firm size: 100-1,000 full-time employees 2. Industry: Manufacturing (NAICS 31-33) or Logistics (NAICS 48-49) 3. Role: Director level or above in supply chain/operations 4. Budget authority: ≥$50k for software purchases 5. Experienced ≥2 major supply chain disruptions in past 24 months

Weighting Plan: - Manufacturing: 60% of sample - Logistics: 40% of sample - Geographic: Proportional to GDP contribution by region - Firm size: Stratified by 100-500 vs 501-1,000 employees

Data Collection: - Method: Online survey with screening questions - Field period: 3 weeks - Incentive: $100 donation to charity of choice per completion - Quality checks: Attention checks, completion time monitoring, IP duplication check

Key Metrics to Collect: 1. Current tool usage and pain points 2. Willingness to pay for integrated solution vs. point tools 3. Feature priority ranking (prediction accuracy vs. workflow automation vs. reporting) 4. Implementation timeline tolerance 5. Decision-making process and stakeholders

Focus Group Design

  • Format: 4 virtual focus groups, 8 participants each
  • Duration: 90 minutes
  • Topics: Product concept reaction, pricing sensitivity, integration requirements
  • Analysis: Thematic analysis of transcripts, sentiment coding
